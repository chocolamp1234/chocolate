But — waiting for you there was a new assailant. In the tense silence of the interview, “the other applicant” lurking inside your body quietly, or not-so-quietly, decides it’s time to speak up.

Don’t panic. How you handle what comes next can actually leave the interviewer more impressed with your “crisis management skills.” Today, we bring you a practical — and admittedly slightly ridiculous — complete guide to handling “sounds” and “situations” that arise mid-interview.

    • Diagnosis: Which “Type” Are You?

    Before diving into the countermeasures, first figure out which type you are. Your type determines which strategy below suits you best.

    • The Thunder God (loud and proud) — the moment it drops, the room’s air freezes solid. Your cover-up skills are a matter of life and death.
    • The Ninja (silent operator) — no sound, but the “job” definitely got done. This one’s a battle fought entirely with facial muscles.
    • The Silent Firework (slow burn hybrid) — quiet at first, then gradually reveals its true power. The most advanced-level case to handle.

    Once you know your type, move on to the countermeasures below.

    One: The Psychological Battle When It Happens — The Art of Playing It Off

    For all you Thunder Gods out there, here’s your first-response playbook for the instant the sound drops.

    The chair-creak disguise. The moment the sound happens, immediately pull your chair back and, with a puzzled expression, mutter “huh, this chair leg makes noise…” and try to reproduce it a few more times. The key is to fully commit to blaming the innocent chair. Even if it’s a little over the top, interviewers rarely push further.

    Synchronizing with a cough (advanced technique). A high-level maneuver where you time the sound to land exactly on a cough. Pull it off and no one notices a thing. Fail, and you get something like “*PFFT* (ahem!)” or worse, “Ahem… *pfffft*… ahem-ahem!?” with the timing off, drawing even more attention than if you’d done nothing. Do not attempt this one without practice.

    The new weapon — blame the ambient noise. A combo move where you pin it on the interviewer’s laptop notification sound, construction noise outside, or the ventilation fan. Slip in a casual “oh, sounds like something’s going on outside” and you might get a psychological noise-canceling effect.

    Two: Dealing With the Silent-But-Deadly “Stealth Attack”

    What Ninjas and Silent Fireworks fear most isn’t the sound — it’s the “atmosphere.”

    An iron will and an unmoving face. Even if you lock eyes with the interviewer, commit to the expression that says “I know nothing, the composition of the air simply changed.” The instant your face betrays panic, you’ve lost. Steady your breathing and press on with the conversation like nothing happened.

    Pin it on the outside world. Glance toward the window and let a faint “is there construction going on out there or something?” vibe leak into the room. But use this move sparingly — glance at the window every five minutes and the interviewer starts to get suspicious for a completely different reason.

    For reference, interviewer reactions can be roughly ranked as follows.

    • Level 1: Total denial (the most common pattern — people are surprisingly good at pretending not to notice)
    • Level 2: Eyes dart for a split second (stay calm here and you’re safe)
    • Level 3: A cough to reset the room (an unspoken “we don’t talk about this” truce forms)
    • Level 4: A pointed glance at the ventilation fan (a bit awkward, but not fatal)
    • Level 5: Both of you burst out laughing and can’t stop (this can actually end on a surprisingly good note)

    Three: Just Own It — Turning It Into a “Crisis Management” Self-PR Moment

    If you decide there’s no covering this one up, embracing it head-on is a perfectly legitimate strategy too.

    Give a crisp, cheerful “Excuse me!” and a quick bow. Don’t ramble on with excuses — pivoting smoothly and quickly to the next topic is exactly what earns respect here. It’s a bit of a stretch to spin it as “someone who can apologize and adapt swiftly when the unexpected happens,” but interviewers are human too. Land the joke well, and the room might actually loosen up because of it.

    That said, never — under any circumstances — use the following phrases.

    • “That was intentional.” (Intentional to what end, exactly?)
    • “Just testing.” (Testing what, nobody will ever know)
    • “I love you.” (This is an unreasonable leap in logic)

    Four: Solve It With Physics — Pre-Interview “Gas Relief” and Prevention

    Everything so far has been damage control after the fact. Now let’s switch to prevention — this, frankly, is the most important part.

    Right before you enter the interview room, a quick bathroom stop with some light “gas-relief massage” (the classic clockwise “no” character motion on your belly) can help release some of the internal pressure ahead of time. Rub your stomach clockwise in the shape of the Japanese character “の.”

    There’s also a condition called aerophagia, where nervousness makes you unconsciously swallow air — often the real source of the gas buildup. Before your interview, try keeping your mouth closed and breathing slowly through your nose to settle your breathing rhythm.

    A Word of Wisdom from the Monk

    Flatulence is impermanent. What must come out, comes out. Accept things as they are. That said — better prepared than sorry. Feel free to lean on the tools in the next chapter, too.

    If an interview falls apart over a fart, it probably wasn’t meant to be anyway — that’s half a joke, and half completely serious. If you have enough breathing room to stay unshaken and even laugh it off, that mishap might actually come across as endearing, human warmth.
