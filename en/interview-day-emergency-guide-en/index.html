How to Avoid Soiling Yourself on the Way to a Job Interview [The Complete Guide]

The complete guide to keeping your stomach in check on interview day. From what to eat the night before to breathing tec

The morning of your interview, on the train, that “feeling” strikes out of nowhere. Of course it happens at the exact moment you’re the most nervous you’ve ever been in your life — your gut is, if nothing else, brutally honest. Today, as your resident monk (and former job-hunting virgin myself), I’m bringing you a practical guide to making it from your front door to the interview room without… incident. Go ahead and laugh. But remember: there but for the grace of the porcelain throne go you.

    • One: Manage Your Diet the Day Before — Don’t Plant a Bomb in Your Gut

    Overindulging the night before an interview is like setting a time bomb for the following morning. Watch out for these three culprits in particular.

    • Greasy food — takes forever to digest and irritates your intestines along the way
    • Dairy — some people have a mild lactose intolerance that turns into a diarrhea trigger
    • Caffeine and alcohol — both send your gut’s peristalsis into overdrive

    “Grilled meat for good luck the night before” is, specifically for job interviews, a terrible idea. Stick to something gentle on the stomach the night before — udon, rice porridge, that sort of thing.

    Two: A Light, Easy-to-Digest Breakfast on the Day

    Skip breakfast and you invite a different enemy — excess stomach acid and a foggy brain, both courtesy of an empty stomach. But eat too much and your intestines kick into gear. The sweet spot is “70% full” with an easy-to-digest menu.

    • A small bowl of rice + miso soup + grilled fish
    • Banana + yogurt (skip the yogurt if you know dairy doesn’t agree with you)
    • Warm tea or hot water to wake your organs up gently

    Like copying sutras, breakfast should be handled “calmly and carefully.” Avoid chugging a cold drink — it shocks your intestines awake in the worst way.

    Three: The Pre-Departure “Ritual” — A Trip to the Bathroom

    This one’s blunt, but it works: the single most effective countermeasure is “go before you go.” Even if you don’t feel the urge, always set aside time to sit in front of the toilet before you leave. Think of it as a ritual. Like zazen, going through the motions settles the mind — and, in this case, the gut.

    Aim to do this 15 minutes before you leave the house. Cut it too close and the resulting rush of panic tends to summon the very urge you were trying to avoid.

    Five: Build Yourself a “Bathroom Map” Along Your Route

    This is a “field skill” I developed back during my own job hunt. Before interview day, use a map app to note down at least three bathroom locations along the route from your home to the interview venue.

    • The bathroom inside the ticket gates at your nearest station
    • The bathroom at your transfer station
    • Convenience stores or cafes near the venue itself (your emergency “safe house”)

    Just knowing “I can make it that far and I’ll be fine” does an astonishing amount to dial down nerve-induced stomach pain. Better prepared than sorry, as they say.

    Seven: Deep Breathing to Steady Your Nervous System

    Nerves push your sympathetic nervous system into overdrive, which throws your intestines out of rhythm — the true identity behind that “my stomach always hurts right before interviews” phenomenon. Before you reach the venue, ideally somewhere private, try this breathing technique.

    1. Slowly inhale through your nose for 4 seconds
    2. Hold your breath for 7 seconds
    3. Slowly exhale through your mouth for 8 seconds

    Repeat this three times and your parasympathetic nervous system takes over, calming your mind — and your stomach — to a surprising degree. Simple but powerful, this technique shares its roots with Zen breathing practice.

    To steady your breath is to steady your mind. And when the mind is steady, so is the stomach.
