“Wait, Which Matters More Right Now — My Life or the Job Offer?” The Correct Way to Handle an Earthquake Mid-Interview (and What You’re Really Thinking)

What happens if an earthquake strikes in the middle of your interview? From the panicked inner monologue to the correct

Interviews have “leak” countermeasures. They have “fart” countermeasures. But surely, some readers might be thinking, nobody needs “earthquake” countermeasures too. And yet — better prepared than sorry. Today, let’s talk about the ultimate worst-case scenario.

Picture this. You’re mid-flow, passionately delivering your self-PR, when your phone and the office’s alarm system blare in unison. “Earthquake Early Warning.” And in the next instant, the floor starts to shake.

Obviously, protecting your life comes first — that goes without saying. And yet some small corner of your brain will still whisper, “Wait, does the interview continue? Pause? Does this affect my score?” That’s just being human. Today we’ll cover the correct way to handle this ultimate-pressure scenario, seriously — with the occasional joke, because let’s be honest.

    • Diagnosis: What’s Your Type When an Earthquake Hits?

    Before we get into it, let’s diagnose your personal “shake tolerance” type.

    • The Roly-Poly (instant reflex) — the second you feel it, a spinal reflex sends you diving under the desk. An outstanding survival instinct, as far as animals go.
    • The Statue (total freeze) — your mind goes blank and you can’t move a muscle. No ill intent, but from the outside it might just look like nerves of steel.
    • The Caretaker (people-first) — you worry about the interviewer, or whoever’s on the other side of the camera, before yourself. Sweet, but please secure your own safety first.

    One: The Earthquake Experience — Inner Monologues and Chaos on the Ground

    The “now is not the time for your motivation statement” championship. You’ll fight the urge to blurt out something like, “My strength is… (*shake*) …staying calm under an earthquake!” the instant it hits. Logically it makes zero sense as a pitch, but somehow, when nerves and panic mix together, it wants to come out anyway.

    For online interviews. Both of you dive under your respective desks on camera, and suddenly there are no humans left in frame. A few seconds later, a surreal voices-only exchange kicks off: “[Name], are you okay…?” with nobody actually visible.

    For in-person interviews. Three seconds after being told “please have a seat,” interviewer and applicant alike dive under the table together. All the earlier tension evaporates, replaced by a bizarre new sense of camaraderie.

    By the way, there’s apparently a ranking of intrusive thoughts that flash through people’s minds mid-shake (results may vary by individual).

    • 3rd place: “Wait, does this affect my results?”
    • 2nd place: “Am I still getting reimbursed for today’s transportation?”
    • 1st place: “Please, suit, do not wrinkle.”

    It’s a little tasteless, sure, but everyone’s mind wanders there for at least a second. Don’t beat yourself up for thinking it. Just… maybe don’t say it out loud.

    Two: [Seriously Now] Protecting Your Life — The Correct Action Manual, Phase by Phase

    From here, let’s get genuinely serious and cover disaster-prep knowledge that actually matters. Even in the unusual setting of a job interview, the basics don’t change.

    1. While the shaking is happening. Set aside your pride, and any expectations about the job offer, just for a moment. Cover your head with your bag and get under the desk immediately. Don’t forget to keep distance from windows and any furniture that could tip over.

    2. Once the shaking stops. First, secure your evacuation route. Doors can warp and jam shut, so open one as soon as the shaking stops. After that, follow the venue’s instructions and any building announcements, and act calmly. Moving on your own judgment is risky.

    3. What comes next. For in-person interviews, just follow the interviewer’s lead. For online interviews, it’s proper etiquette to say something before you leave. Here are some ready-to-use phrases.

    • “There’s been a significant earthquake, so I’ll step away briefly to ensure my safety. I’ll reach out again once things have settled.”
    • (In the chat) “Stepping away briefly due to the earthquake. Not to worry — I’ll follow up shortly.”
    • For in-person: A simple “Understood. I’ll follow your lead” is plenty. No need to say more.

    There’s also prep you can do before you even head to the interview. Take a quick look at the hazard map for the area around the venue, and check the evacuation route (location of emergency exits) as soon as you arrive. These habits are useful well beyond interviews.

    Three: “Godlike Response” Stories from Interviewers and Applicants (Comedy-Sketch Style)

    Let’s look at some real (or at least entirely plausible) tales of graceful handling.

    The applicant response that earns bonus points. Someone who stays calm and says “let’s make sure everyone’s safe first” without panicking has, in that single sentence, basically proven their crisis-management skills. The mental fortitude to calmly restart the interview afterward with “sorry for the moment of panic earlier, let’s pick back up” is itself an impressive selling point.

    The interviewer response that reveals company culture. Any company whose interviewer can instantly say “forget the job offer, your life matters more — evacuate now!” is almost certainly a genuinely good place to work. There are even (true) stories of applicants striking up small talk during evacuation, hitting it off with the interviewer, and getting hired on the spot.

    On the flip side, here are some rookie mistakes to avoid.

    • Dutifully continuing your self-PR speech even as the shaking continues (this reads as zero crisis-management skill)
    • Asking “so, when will I hear back about the results?” the second you finish evacuating (understandable impulse, but read the room)
    • Sprinting straight for the emergency exit without a second thought for the interviewer (understandable, but spare a glance for the people around you)

    A Word of Wisdom from the Monk

    The earth shakes, and so does the heart. This too is the way of nature. And yet, preparation helps build a heart that does not shake. Do not panic — but do not let your guard down, either. That is what matters most.

    Five: In Conclusion — “Your Life” Matters More Than “The Job Offer”

    No matter how well the interview was going, none of it means anything without your life intact. Obvious as that sounds, it’s surprisingly easy to forget in the white-hot tension of an interview.

    And how someone handles a disaster reveals a lot — both about their true character, and about a company’s real culture (or lack thereof) when it comes to safety. If an earthquake ever does strike mid-interview, consider it a rare, unfiltered glimpse of the company’s true face — the one you’ll never find in a brochure.
